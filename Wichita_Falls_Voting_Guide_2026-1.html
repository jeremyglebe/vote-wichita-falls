What to bring to vote

Accepted photo ID

Bring the physical document. A U.S. passport card counts, just like a passport book. Accepted forms are: a Texas DPS driver license, personal ID card, election identification certificate or handgun license; a U.S. passport book or card; U.S. military photo ID; or a U.S. citizenship certificate with your photo.

Your ageExpired Texas license, passport card or other accepted photo ID
18–69Current, or expired no more than four years when presented to vote.
70 or olderExpired for any length of time, provided it is otherwise valid.

U.S. citizenship certificates do not expire. If you do not possess and cannot reasonably obtain an accepted photo ID, you may use an approved supporting document and complete a Reasonable Impediment Declaration at the polls. Examples include a current utility bill, bank statement or voter registration certificate. VoteTexas.gov · accepted photo IDs, expiration and alternatives

Where paper guides are permitted

You may carry and consult a handwritten list, printed voter guide or marked sample ballot inside the polling place, including at your voting station / in the booth, for your own reference. This guide’s personal checklist can be used that way. Texas Secretary of State · written materials in voting stations, slide 24 · Texas Secretary of State · printed notes and phone rules

Personal reference is permitted; campaigning to other voters is restricted. Do not hand out copies, display the guide as a campaign sign, or use it to persuade other voters inside the marked 100-foot boundary from polling-place entrances or the marked 20-foot boundary around designated curbside-voting spaces. Election officials determine whether conduct amounts to electioneering. VoteTexas.gov · electioneering boundaries, phones and recording · Texas Secretary of State · written materials in voting stations, slide 24

Phones: keep them put away in the voting room. Photos and sound / video recording are prohibited within 100 feet of a voting station. Print your choices beforehand. VoteTexas.gov · electioneering boundaries, phones and recording

How to read the tags

Tags come before each description. “Nazis / far right” includes documented white-nationalist connections, Nazi-style propaganda and Christian-nationalist politics. It does not mean every tagged person belongs to a literal Nazi organization. Each connection is named and sourced.

NAZIS / FAR RIGHT

Direct rhetoric, historical staff ties, group endorsements and broader political characterizations are distinguished in the evidence notes.

TECH BROS / TECH POLICY

Tech-billionaire money is separate from support for a particular data center. An ordinary technical job does not establish a billionaire alliance.

ZIONISM / ISRAEL SUPPORT

Political support for Israel, arms or anti-BDS rules. Never inferred from Jewish identity. Mixed positions are spelled out, including Talarico’s.

TALARICO / HINOJOSA / DSA ENDORSEMENT

A formal endorsement is separate from a campaign donation, shared appearance, party label or support from another progressive organization.

TRUMP / MAGA

A current endorsement, explicit self-description or specific Trump-team relationship. Republican affiliation alone is not sufficient.

PAXTON / ABBOTT CONNECTION

Appointment, employment, advisory service, endorsement or a specifically identified policy alignment. Dates and connection type matter.
